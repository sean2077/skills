# Human-facing Specification Writing

Write a coherent document that a reviewer, implementer, or tester can use.

Establish the audience, review object, status, and current versus target behavior. Follow the project's terminology, templates, and source ownership.

Preserve identifiers, exact values, compatibility constraints, and normative strength. Expose material assumptions, conflicts, and open decisions. Seek clarification where it changes the document's meaning; useful drafting can continue around visible gaps.

Lead with the intended outcome and main behavior or architecture flow. Explain decision-bearing responsibilities, interfaces, failure/recovery behavior, and observable acceptance. Use non-goals where they prevent a plausible misunderstanding.

Define concepts once and link to their authority. Summarize executable/generated contracts for readers while retaining exact detail that this document owns.

Compare unresolved material choices or choices the user has asked to reconsider. Preserve settled decisions and concise rationale; link detailed decision history where useful.

Split documents when audiences, ownership, approval, or lifecycles differ. Keep working transcripts and verification logs in their owning records rather than the reader narrative.

Before handoff, reconcile terms, links, diagrams, constraints, main flow, and acceptance. Distinguish evidence, assumptions, and open questions, and preserve the document's actual approval status.
